The Sword Saint's Last Spring

For most of his life, the Sword Saint had been surrounded by swords.

There were three in particular that had followed him wherever his name was known: Heaven's Edge, Moonfall, and Silent Dawn. Each had a different history, and each had been responsible for victories that became the subject of countless stories throughout the Murim.

In his later years, however, the Sword Saint stopped using them.

He retired to a small house on a quiet mountain and spent most of his days tending to a bonsai tree that stood beside his window. The tree was old and strangely shaped. Its trunk had grown crooked over the years, and one of its branches had long since withered. To anyone else, it looked like a plant that should have been discarded.

The Sword Saint never saw it that way.

Every morning, he watered it, removed the dead leaves, and carefully trimmed its branches. He treated the tree with more patience than he had ever shown toward his own disciples.

One spring morning, a young swordsman came to visit him. He had heard countless stories about the Sword Saint and had climbed the mountain hoping to witness one of the legendary swords.

Instead, he found the old man sitting beside the window with a pair of pruning shears in his hand.

"Senior, may I ask you something?"

The old man continued trimming the bonsai. "What is it?"

"Why did you abandon the sword?"

The old man smiled faintly. "I did not abandon it. I simply realized that there are things more important than winning a fight."

The young swordsman looked toward the three swords resting against the wall. "Then why keep them?"

The old man followed his gaze. "Because I have spent too many years carrying them to throw them away now."

The young man laughed. "That is not a very satisfying answer."

"It is the only honest one I have."

Before the young man could respond, footsteps came from outside. Dozens of men had gathered at the entrance to the house.

Their leader announced that they had come to claim the three swords of the Sword Saint. The old man recognized the crest on their robes immediately. They belonged to a powerful martial clan that had spent years searching for his whereabouts.

The old man sighed. "I was hoping I would never have to use them again."

He stood slowly and walked toward the three swords. The young swordsman watched in silence.

The old man picked up Heaven's Edge first. The moment the blade left its sheath, the atmosphere on the mountain changed.

The battle that followed lasted only a few minutes. The Sword Saint did not fight with the fury of his youth. His movements were quiet and economical. Every strike had a purpose, and every step placed him exactly where he needed to be.

When the final opponent fell, the old man returned the sword to its sheath. No one had died. The Sword Saint had defeated every man without taking a single life.

He turned toward his house. Then he stopped.

During the battle, one of the attackers had fallen against the window and knocked over the bonsai. The clay pot had broken. The tree lay on the ground, its roots exposed.

The old man's expression changed. He walked over and carefully lifted it from the soil. For a long moment, he said nothing.

Then he looked at the defeated swordsmen. "You came here because you wanted my swords. You should have understood what they were meant to protect."

The young swordsman helped him repair the tree and place it into a new pot.

That evening, the Sword Saint sat beside the bonsai until sunset.

"Master," the young swordsman asked, "what is so important about that tree?"

The old man looked at it for a long time before answering. "I planted it on the day I decided that I had killed enough people."

He placed a hand against the trunk. "Every year since then, I have watched it grow. It reminds me that life does not become meaningless simply because it is small, weak, or easily overlooked."

The young swordsman glanced at the three legendary swords. "Then what are the swords for?"

The old man smiled. "Perhaps that is something you will have to decide for yourself."

Years later, the Sword Saint disappeared from the Murim. Some claimed that he died peacefully on the mountain. Others believed that he left the martial world and traveled under another name. No one ever discovered the truth.

The three swords remained in his house. So did the bonsai.

The tree continued to grow, slowly and unevenly, producing new branches where the old ones had been cut away.

Those who visited the mountain afterward often wondered why a man who had once been feared throughout the Murim had devoted the final years of his life to something so fragile.

Perhaps the answer was simple.

The three swords represented everything the Sword Saint had once been.
The bonsai represented everything he had finally learned to protect.
